How the score works

Every factor is bounded 0–1, so the product is a real 0–100 index that is comparable across chambers and across days. Scores cluster well below 100 by construction — a 55 is a strong result, not a mediocre one.

The formula, and what it deliberately leaves out
  • "Seats in doubt" — every House seat is contested every two years, so this is not a count of elections. From the selected party's point of view each seat is sorted by forecast win probability into near-certain (95% or better), in doubt (between 5% and 95%), or out of reach (5% or less). Only the middle group can realistically change the arithmetic, and only those are ranked here. The Senate figure also folds in the 65 seats not on the 2026 ballot, since they still count toward 51.
  • Closeness — 4 × p × (1 − p), peaking at a 50/50 race. Raw win probability would reward near-certain outcomes, where money changes nothing.
  • Control tier — every seat is ranked by closeness, then banded by where it falls relative to the number still needed for a majority. Seats a party already holds are included, so defending a coin-flip seat counts exactly as much as winning one.
  • Funding need — half from how far the candidate is below what this seat historically costs, half from how they compare to their opponent. Expressed as a share rather than a ratio so an unfunded opponent can't send it to infinity.
  • Outside spending — independent expenditures adjust funding need by ±25–50%, discounted by half because outside groups pay market ad rates while candidates get the lowest unit charge. Bounded deliberately: it's money you cannot affect, cumulative spending rather than cash in hand, and heavily back-loaded.
  • Viability — damps candidates who have raised almost nothing. Without it, a campaign with $0 raised scores highest of all, which is backwards: that's usually a non-campaign, not a bargain.
  • Not included: dark money and issue advocacy that never triggers a filing, party coordinated spending, candidate quality, or anything about the candidates' politics beyond party.
